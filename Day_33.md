💡 Key Learnings

1. Read beyond the headline.
A headline can be connected to a real story while still exaggerating what the evidence actually proves. The first few paragraphs often reveal a very different reality.

2. Source reliability and headline accuracy are different questions.
A dramatic claim can sound convincing even when the source is weak—and even a relatively credible source can publish an exaggerated headline.

3. Strong emotions are a reason to slow down.
Fear, anger, urgency, and excitement can push us to click, comment, or share before checking the evidence.

4. Emotional manipulation often hides inside specific words.
Words such as “stunned,” “terrified,” “chaos,” “proves,” and “they don't want you to know” can shape our reaction before we process the facts.

5. Always ask who benefits from your reaction.
Who created the content? What do they gain from your click or share? Is another reliable source reporting the same facts?
